# Research and implementation decision

Researched October 1, 2026. This is a comparison for an interactive, smooth, excavatable Bevy terrain, not a claim that one method wins every terrain benchmark. Primary author pages, papers and official implementations were used. Full text was inspected where accessible; publisher abstracts and official descriptions were used for other entries. Dates below are publication/submission dates, not search-engine crawl dates.

## Recommendation

For this finite interactive scene: **erode a heightfield once, convert it to a sparse cached 3D signed density field, edit the cached samples, and rasterize local Surface Nets meshes**. Store shape separately from appearance. Use world-space material detail for features too small to justify more triangles.

The implementation is a working baseline for this combination, not a reproduction of the newest erosion paper. It does not implement an octree or claim a measured advantage over every competing mesher. The recommendation follows from the task's need for frequent local edits, smooth natural shapes and straightforward integration with Bevy's lighting. Benchmark this baseline before replacing its storage or renderer.

## Terrain shape and erosion

| Primary research | Contribution and tradeoff | Decision for this project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| McDonald & Cordonnier, **Stochastic Geomorphological Transport for Terrain Erosion Simulation**, SIGGRAPH/TOG, July 2026. [Paper/project](https://erosiv.studio/publications/stochastic-geomorphological-transport), [official core code](https://github.com/erosiv/geotransport) | Transport with momentum conservation connects particle methods to conservation laws and produces coherent basins, braided channels, meanders and deposits. It is a strong recent direction for physical realism. The reference code uses CUDA and covers the core integrator; the erosion implementation is separate. | Best candidate for a future offline/GPU landscape generator when these landforms are required. Current droplets are a heuristic, not this solver, and do not reproduce its scale invariance or physical validation. |
| Schott et al., **Terrain Amplification using Multi-scale Erosion**, SIGGRAPH/TOG, 2024. [Adobe research](https://research.adobe.com/publication/terrain-amplification-using-multi-scale-erosion/), [official code](https://github.com/H-Schott/MultiScaleErosion) | Erosion at multiple scales combines stream-power, thermal and hillslope effects with hydrological correction. Noise alone does not preserve drainage structure while adding detail. | Prefer this approach for large authored landscapes and resolution amplification. Current implementation uses one grid and hydraulic plus thermal passes; it omits their multi-scale breaching and retargeting. |
| Tzathas et al., **Physically-based Analytical Erosion for Fast Terrain Generation**, Eurographics, 2024. [Author paper](https://www-sop.inria.fr/reves/Basilic/2024/TGSC24/Analytical_Terrains_EG.pdf) | Analytical stream-power solutions make landscape age a parameter, with coupled hydrology solved using accelerated iterations. Includes hillslope and landslide treatment. | Strong option for geological-scale mountain structure and authoring. More appropriate than scaling a small water simulation to continent size. Not implemented here. |
| Grenier et al., **Real-time Terrain Enhancement with Controlled Procedural Patterns**, CGF, 2024. [Paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/cgf.14992) | Slope-oriented phasor patterns add cascading ravines at fine scales without a global erosion simulation. | Suitable for distant/detail amplification. Keep procedural appearance detail separate from collision geometry. Our shader noise is not their phasor method. |
| Yang et al., **Unerosion: Simulating Terrain Evolution Back in Time**, SCA/CGF, 2024. [Author paper](https://cs.purdue.edu/cgvlab/www/resources/papers/Yang-CGF-2024-Unerosion.pdf) | Reverse landscape evolution supports controlled reconstruction using fluvial and thermal processes. | Useful for matching an existing landform and geological authoring; unnecessary for a first forward-generated editable scene. |
| Huftier et al., **Terrain Synthesis and Authoring Based on Iso-Contours**, CGF, 2026. [Publisher paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cgf.70389) | Nested contours give compact terrain authoring with direct slope and shape control; a heightfield can be reconstructed for erosion. | An alternative to noise for intentional mesas, coastlines and ridges. Contours describe surface elevation; a separate 3D representation is still needed for excavations. |

Implemented shape pipeline: domain-warped ridged Perlin noise establishes relief around a prescribed valley. Inertial droplets erode a weighted neighbourhood, carry sediment and deposit it when capacity falls or the droplet terminates. Thermal transfers relax slopes above a talus threshold. Accumulated droplet visits drive a visual wetness mask. Parameters remain available in `Settings` rather than being described as universal physical constants. This is plausible local morphology; it does not certify geological consistency or full drainage connectivity.

Other real-world processes matter when the target landscape requires them: tectonic uplift and lithology for mountain ranges; debris flow, rockfall and landslides for fans and scarps; aeolian transport for dunes; glacial erosion for U-shaped valleys; karst dissolution for natural cave networks; vegetation, weathering and sedimentation for surface cover. The prototype models hydraulic and thermal effects only. A shader layer pattern is not a simulation of sedimentary geology.

## Smooth voxels and digging

| Primary research / implementation | Strength and limitation | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carrera et al., **Dual Contouring of Signed Distance Data**, March 2026, revised May 2026. [Paper/full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00157v2) | Optimizes vertex placement from discrete SDF samples without requiring analytic gradients, emphasizing sharp-feature reconstruction. Reconstruction quality is not the same benchmark as interactive chunk remeshing throughput. | Promising upgrade for crisp strata and tool cuts. Validate runtime, boundary compatibility and the assumptions on metric distance data; the current field is not an exact SDF. Not implemented. |
| Ju et al., **Dual Contouring of Hermite Data**, SIGGRAPH, 2002. [Author publication page and paper](https://www.cs.rice.edu/~jwarren/research/index.html) | Hermite edge intersections and normals feed a quadratic-error fit, preserving sharp features and enabling octree contour simplification. Numerical conditioning and ambiguous topology need care. | Prefer a robust manifold/intersection-aware variant for sharply structured rock. Smooth soil does not initially require a QEF solver. |
| Gibson, **Constrained Elastic Surface Nets**, MICCAI 1998 / [MERL report 1999](https://www.merl.com/publications/TR99-24), and the modern [fast-surface-nets implementation](https://github.com/bonsairobo/fast-surface-nets-rs) | The original method relaxes a constrained surface graph. The crate instead implements *naive* Surface Nets: average edge crossings, one vertex per active cell, gradient normals and chunk-friendly boundary ownership. These are related methods, not identical algorithms. | Selected existing crate. It provides smooth natural surfaces with little integration code. Sharp features soften, and ambiguous fields are not universally manifold. |
| Lengyel, **Voxel-Based Terrain for Real-Time Virtual Simulations / Transvoxel**, 2010. [Author algorithm and dissertation](https://transvoxel.org/) | Transition cells connect marching-cubes meshes across a 2:1 resolution boundary. This addresses LOD cracks rather than sparse storage or erosion. | Use when adding distance LOD to a marching-cubes terrain. Its lookup tables cannot simply be attached to Surface Nets; meshing and transition strategy must be selected together. |
| Amanatides & Woo, **A Fast Voxel Traversal Algorithm for Ray Tracing**, 1987; [DeadlockCode's implementation supplied in the request](https://github.com/DeadlockCode/voxel_ray_traversal) | DDA visits grid cells along a ray efficiently. The linked project is an educational Vulkan compute renderer and reports that its branchless version was slower on the author's GPU. Cell traversal does not itself reconstruct smooth surfaces. | Valuable for accelerating picking or a later ray renderer. Do not assume branchless traversal or a new custom renderer is automatically faster. No source was copied into this project. |

Digging uses negative-inside samples. Removing a sphere applies `max(field, radius - distance)`; adding material applies `min(field, distance - radius)`. Interpolated samples define a smooth zero contour that can have several surfaces in a vertical column. The original heightfield supplies the starting solid volume and material-depth reference, not the edited topology.

Edits update every overlapping chunk sample, including edge/corner halos. Mesh jobs own immutable snapshots and content revisions. A stale result cannot replace a newer surface. Mesh tasks and accepted uploads are bounded. Chunks are not unloaded/recreated in this finite implementation; a streaming extension would also need lifecycle epochs. Picking evaluates the current edited field, independent of delayed render meshes.

## Sparse octrees and cached SDFs

| Primary research | What it actually solves | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laine & Karras, **Efficient Sparse Voxel Octrees**, 2010. [NVIDIA technical report](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2010-02_Efficient-Sparse-Voxel/laine2010tr1_paper.pdf) | Compact sparse geometry, contour information and efficient hierarchical ray casting. Smooth shading/contours are additional representation choices. The tree itself does not guarantee a smooth editable terrain. | Good for huge sparse ray-rendered scenes. Frequent edits, GPU synchronization and smooth LOD extraction require additional machinery. Not the first storage layer for this scene. |
| Museth, **VDB: High-Resolution Sparse Volumes with Dynamic Topology**, 2013; **NanoVDB**, 2021. [OpenVDB documentation](https://www.openvdb.org/documentation/), [NanoVDB paper](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/prl/nanovdb/nanovdb2021.pdf) | VDB handles sparse dynamic volumes and level-set operations. NanoVDB provides a compact GPU-portable layout, numerical tools and hierarchical DDA. Its 2021 description emphasizes static sparse topology rather than effortless arbitrary topology edits on the GPU. | Strong reference for an eventual hierarchical brick cache. A full C++ VDB integration is unnecessary for the current Rust scene. |
| De Rebotti et al., **Resolution Where It Counts: Hash-based GPU-Accelerated 3D Reconstruction via Variance-Adaptive Voxel Grids**, TOG, December 2025. [Author project](https://rvp-group.github.io/mrhash/), [publication](https://doi.org/10.1145/3777909) | Flat hashed blocks support adaptive TSDF fusion without recursive octree access. The application is reconstruction from observations, not excavatable game terrain. | Supports investigating block hashing as an alternative to an octree. We use a CPU hash of fixed-size chunks; no claim of reproducing their adaptive algorithm or GPU performance. |
| Barbier et al., **Lipschitz Pruning: Hierarchical Simplification of Primitive-Based SDFs**, Eurographics, 2025. [Author paper](https://wbrbr.org/publications/LipschitzPruning/documents/LipschitzPruning_submitted_to_EG25.pdf) | Region-specific pruning reduces evaluation of large analytical CSG trees while preserving valid distance bounds. | Useful if edits remain a large procedural CSG history. We bake edits into cached samples, so evaluation cost does not increase with every previous dig. No pruning tree is needed yet. |
| Ulschmid et al., **NAADF: Globally Illuminated Voxel Worlds Accelerated with Nested Axis-Aligned Distance Fields**, Eurographics, May 2026. [Author research page](https://www.cg.tuwien.ac.at/research/publications/2026/ulschmid-2026-naadf/) | Nested cells and axis-aligned distance caches accelerate voxel ray traversal; the paper also addresses editing and temporal GI reuse. The authors report around 10× traversal gains on their evaluated workloads. These caches are not automatically a smooth signed terrain distance field. | Recent, relevant option for a voxel ray/path renderer. Published speedups cannot be transferred to this rasterized smooth terrain. Treat it as a separate renderer experiment. |

**Cached fields are useful here.** Only chunks around the initial surface are allocated; unallocated solid/empty regions retain the procedural base field. Underground or added chunks are allocated when a brush reaches them. Repeated mesh extraction reads cache memory instead of re-running erosion/noise or replaying an ever-growing brush history. The cache uses f32 samples; quantization and narrow-band compression should follow error measurements rather than guessing.

Important distinction: `y - height(x,z)` has the correct sign but is not Euclidean distance on slopes. Adding noise and sampled CSG does not restore unit-gradient distance. Therefore this project does not call the values safe sphere-tracing step lengths, apply Lipschitz pruning without a bound, or claim metric SDF accuracy. A production distance-based renderer would need re-distancing, verified bounds, or cell-based intersection tests.

## Visual realism

| Primary research | Practical implication | Implementation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mikkelsen, **Surface Gradient–Based Bump Mapping Framework**, JCGT, 2020. [Paper](https://jcgt.org/published/0009/03/04/paper.pdf) | Volume/procedural bump detail can remain coherent without a mesh tangent basis, including triplanar-style workflows. | World-space 3D noise, surface-gradient normal perturbation, pixel-footprint attenuation of bump strength. |
| Heitz & Neyret, **High-Performance By-Example Noise Using a Histogram-Preserving Blending Operator**, HPG, 2018. [Author project](https://eheitzresearch.wordpress.com/722-2/) | Stochastic example texture blending avoids visible repetition while preserving local contrast and texture appearance. | Recommended next material upgrade with measured rock/soil albedo, normals and roughness. Current material is procedural noise; it is not this texture-synthesis method. |
| Lutz & Gilet, **Real-time By-example Texture Synthesis and Filtering Using Local Statistics Exchange**, Eurographics, May 2026. [Publisher paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cgf.70355), [author page](https://www.lirmm.fr/nicolas-lutz/index.html) | Exchanges local statistics to reduce repetition in exemplars containing distinct irregular regions, with a filtering approximation. It preserves the exemplar's structural arrangement. | A recent option for scanned terrain materials whose regional patterns are poorly served by simple stochastic tile blending. Compare the two methods on the selected scans before adoption; neither is implemented here. |
| Hillaire, **A Scalable and Production Ready Sky and Atmosphere Rendering Technique**, EGSR, 2020. [Author paper](https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf) | Lookup tables and multiple-scattering approximation provide practical physical sky illumination and aerial perspective. | Reuse Bevy's atmosphere and environment lighting, plus distance haze, instead of implementing a new sky renderer. |

Appearance is assembled from slope, altitude, original surface depth and erosion-flow masks. Gentle surfaces get soil and patchy grass; steep or deeply excavated surfaces expose rock; high gentle slopes can accumulate snow. Geometric density variation affects silhouettes, while procedural microdetail affects shading only. A more photoreal result needs calibrated material scans, appropriate vegetation and boulders, better regional geology, and lighting matched to the intended biome. Path tracing is not a substitute for those inputs.

## What to measure before scaling up

Run the included benchmark and GPU smoke test first. Record CPU edit time, affected chunks, actual meshing time, end-to-end accepted-job latency, cached field memory, triangle counts and frame times separately. Compare at the same voxel size, camera, seed and brush stroke; warm shader pipelines before measuring GPU frames.

For kilometre-scale worlds, add streaming and LOD with a compatible seam strategy. For expensive initialization, move erosion/cache construction to GPU or bake a generated heightfield. For crisp rock cuts, evaluate robust dual contouring against the current Surface Nets baseline. For a full voxel path tracer, compare SVO/VDB-style HDDA and NAADF on this scene, including cache invalidation and GI history reset after edits. None of those comparisons has been measured by the initial implementation.
